Motion
Proposition ✓ This House believes that homework should be banned.
What you're seeing: Three arguments ranked 1–3 with Reasoning, Evidence, and Significance filled in using the homework motion. The fourth column was brainstormed but crossed out — the team decided it overlapped with Argument 2. Notice each claim is specific, not just a theme.
Claim / Assertion
2
Homework cuts into the sleep and recovery time students need to actually learn.
Reasoning
Sleep is when the brain consolidates what it learned that day. A student who stays up until midnight doing worksheets is actively undermining the learning from that same day.
Evidence
Stanford study (4,300 students): more than 3 hrs/night of homework = higher stress, worse health, less sleep. These were motivated, high-performing kids.
Significance
Millions of students nightly. Kids from lower-income homes have less support & shorter evenings — the harm is bigger there, not smaller.
Claim / Assertion
1
Homework takes up too much student free time without improving learning outcomes.
Reasoning
Free time isn't wasted time — it's when kids develop independence, creativity, and relationships. When homework consumes evenings, those things disappear and so does intrinsic motivation.
Evidence
Harris Cooper meta-analysis: homework has zero measurable benefit for elementary students. For middle school, benefit only appears at small amounts — not the hours most kids face.
Significance
This affects every student in every school — every single evening. A ban doesn't reduce learning; it reclaims the part of childhood that makes learning sustainable.
Claim / Assertion
3
Homework widens the gap between students with support at home and those without.
Reasoning
A student with a tutor, a quiet room, and a parent who can explain the math has a fundamentally different homework experience than one who doesn't. Same assignment, completely different outcome.
Evidence
PISA data: in low-income schools, homework completion rates are significantly lower — not because of effort, but because of environment. The gap it creates is measurable by 8th grade.
Significance
Homework is supposed to help everyone learn equally. Instead it advantages kids who are already advantaged. A ban levels the field — the classroom becomes the only arena that counts.

Teachers can cover material more effectively in class than students can practice alone at home.
Reasoning

Evidence
